"Poop Tracking is Love"

An interview with Yolanda Xing

by Steven Harmon

Recently a colleague shared with me a poop tracking game called Scarab and while there aren't any toilets depicted within the interface of the app, I felt compelled to interview the creator of it since it was designed to be played while on the toilet. What ensued was a lovely conversation about data and dung, of poo and personal time, of crap and connection.


Follow Yolanda here: @yolandaxxing


Q: Why track poop in a game?

Steven: All right. Thank you so much, Yolanda Xing, for coming. It's great to have you. Toilet Game Studies covers games that feature toilets, but it's also interested in games that are designed to be played whilst on the toilet.

And that's why I thought Scarib was a great, choice to interview, and I'm glad you're on the interview. So I guess first off, the big question in the room is, what inspired you to make a poop tracking game? Were the existing poop tracking apps not gamified, social, or engaging enough for your taste?

Yolanda Xing: It came from a very feminist view, because I've always tracked my period with the Apple Health app. And, often when women had their period, their poo will get very watery. It's almost like a diarrhea. And I feel like it's should be recorded somewhere.

And when I see all the categories in Apple Health, it has everything from the amount of water you drink to even the time you wash your hand. It has all that very detailed categories, but it doesn't have a section for poo. And so I found that to be very surprising and so why not just make it myself?

It's more important to have your poo consistently. If you have your poo every morning or every time before you go to bed. And so having that rhythm is more important than how much poo you actually do on daily basis, if that makes sense.
